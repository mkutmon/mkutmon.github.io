<div class="a-hero">
  <p class="hello">Hello!</p>
  <p class="tagline">I am an Associate Professor at the <strong><a href="https://www.maastrichtuniversity.nl/research/maastricht-centre-systems-biology-and-bioinformatics">Maastricht Centre for Systems Biology and Bioinformatics (MaCSBio)</a></strong> at Maastricht University, working on <strong>network biology</strong>: connecting data across biological scales to understand disease mechanisms.</p>
</div>

<p>I develop computational approaches that integrate diverse types of data – from multi-omics to clinical and imaging data – across biological scales, from molecular interactions to physiological systems and the whole body. Biological pathways, networks and disease maps form the backbone of this work, providing a structured way to connect heterogeneous data and to interpret it in the context of disease mechanisms.</p>

<p>I apply these approaches to diverse complex, multi-system contexts like </p>
<div class="a-tags">
  <span class="a-tag">post-COVID</span><span class="a-tag">inflammatory skin diseases</span><span class="a-tag">pancreatic cancer</span><span class="a-tag">diet intervention studies</span><span class="a-tag">rare diseases</span><span class="a-tag">kidney function</span><span class="a-tag">cardiovascular disease</span><span class="a-tag">immune memory and vaccination</span>
</div>

<h2><i class="fas fa-fw fa-users" aria-hidden="true"></i> Community &amp; collaboration</h2>
<p>My research is interdisciplinary by nature. I work closely with clinicians, experimental researchers and fellow computational scientists across a broad range of application domains, combining their expertise and data with network-based approaches. I enjoy bringing people together: I regularly organise conferences, workshops and community events that connect researchers across disciplines and career stages – for example within <a href="https://www.iscb.org">ISCB</a>, BioSB and <a href="https://www.widsworldwide.org">Women in Data Science (WiDS)</a> Maastricht.</p>
